The real numbers have a gap: x² + 1 = 0 has no solution among them. Complex
numbers close it by declaring a number i with i² = −1 and then carrying on with
ordinary algebra. Every complex number is then a + bi, with a the real part and
b the imaginary part, and addition and multiplication follow the usual rules
with i² replaced by −1 wherever it appears.

The word "imaginary" is a historical accident and has done real damage. It
suggests these numbers are fictitious in a way that, say, negative numbers are
not — but negative numbers were once resisted on exactly the same grounds. The
practical answer is that complex numbers describe physical things routinely:
alternating-current impedance, wave amplitude and phase together, and the
quantum wavefunction are all naturally complex, and the arithmetic is what makes
those calculations tractable.

Two genuine differences from the reals are worth knowing. There is no ordering:
asking whether 2 + 3i is greater than 3 + 2i has no answer consistent with
arithmetic, and the question simply does not apply. What you *can* compare is
the modulus |a + bi| = √(a² + b²), the distance from the origin when the number
is drawn as the point (a, b) — which is why it is √(3² + 4²) = 5 for 3 + 4i,
not 3 + 4. That picture, the complex plane, turns complex arithmetic into plane
geometry and is where the polar form comes from.
