Boolean algebra reduces every digital circuit's behavior to combinations of just true and false, and George Boole's original insight was that this two-valued algebra follows its own consistent set of laws -- AND and OR distribute over each other in specific ways, and De Morgan's laws let NOT be pushed through an AND or OR, converting one into the other. None of this is abstract bookkeeping: those laws are exactly what lets an engineer take a logic function specified with AND/OR/NOT gates and mechanically rewrite it into an equivalent function built entirely from NAND gates (or entirely from NOR gates) instead.

A truth table is what makes a logic function's specification airtight in the first place: for n binary inputs, it lists all 2^n possible input combinations and the correct output for each one, with nothing left implicit. Once a function is pinned down this exhaustively, Boolean algebra's laws can be applied with total confidence that the rewritten expression is truly equivalent, not just similar.

NAND and NOR's special status is what makes all of this matter industrially. Because AND, OR, and NOT can each be constructed using nothing but NAND gates (or, separately, nothing but NOR gates), a chip manufacturer never needs to fabricate multiple different gate types side by side -- an entire complex digital circuit, however large, can be built from repeated instances of one single, thoroughly optimized gate design. That uniformity is a major reason modern manufacturing can produce chips containing billions of gates reliably and cheaply: variety in gate type would mean variety in manufacturing steps, and manufacturing variety is exactly what modern chip fabrication works hardest to eliminate.
