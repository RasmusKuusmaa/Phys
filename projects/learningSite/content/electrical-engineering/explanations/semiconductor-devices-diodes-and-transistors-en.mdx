A p-n junction is where p-type semiconductor (with an excess of mobile positive "holes") meets n-type semiconductor (with an excess of mobile electrons). At the junction itself, electrons and holes diffuse across and recombine, leaving behind a thin region depleted of mobile charge carriers -- the depletion region -- which acts as a barrier to further current flow. Forward bias (positive terminal to the p-side) pushes charge carriers toward the junction and narrows this barrier until current flows easily; reverse bias does the opposite, widening the depletion region and blocking current almost completely. This asymmetry -- easy conduction one way, almost none the other -- is rectification, and it's the diode's entire reason for existing as a device: converting an alternating voltage into a one-directional one, among many other uses.

A bipolar junction transistor takes this same junction physics and builds two of them back to back, sharing a very thin middle region (the base) sandwiched between two outer regions (emitter and collector). The key physical effect is that a small current injected into the thin base region controls, out of proportion to its own size, a much larger current that the transistor allows to flow from emitter to collector -- the base current acts like a valve setting how far the much larger collector current can flow. Because that relationship is continuous and proportional over a range of base currents, the same physical device can either amplify (a small, continuously varying base current produces a proportionally larger, continuously varying collector current -- the transistor's linear/active region) or switch (driving the base current to its extremes forces the transistor fully on or fully off -- saturation and cutoff, the two states every digital logic gate built from transistors actually uses).

It's the same physical device operating in two different regimes, not two different devices, and knowing which regime a particular circuit is using it in is exactly what separates analog amplifier design from digital logic design.
