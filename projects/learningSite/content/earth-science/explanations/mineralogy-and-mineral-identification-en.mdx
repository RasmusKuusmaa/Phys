A mineral's identity is fixed by its internal crystal structure and characteristic chemistry, and every standard field-identification property is really just a different physical consequence of that internal structure -- which is why geologists trust these properties over a mineral's outward appearance, which impurities and weathering can disguise.

Hardness (the Mohs scale) tests how strongly a crystal's internal atomic bonds resist being pulled apart when scratched. Because the scale is built entirely on which of ten reference minerals can scratch which others, it is an ordinal ranking, not an absolute unit of force -- knowing that quartz (7) scratches feldspar (6) tells you their relative order, not how many times stronger one bond is than another.

Cleavage exists because a crystal structure is not equally strong in every direction; certain planes running through the lattice have systematically weaker bonding than others, so the mineral preferentially splits along those specific planes, at specific angles, producing smooth, flat, often shiny break surfaces. A mineral with no such systematic weak plane instead fractures along irregular surfaces when broken -- both cleavage (or its absence, and fracture instead) and the specific angles between cleavage planes are themselves diagnostic, since different minerals have different internal weak-plane geometries.

Streak is often more useful than surface color precisely because surface color is unusually easy to fool: trace amounts of an impurity element substituting into the crystal structure can shift a mineral's outward color dramatically without changing what mineral it fundamentally is. Grinding the mineral into a fine powder (via the streak plate) reduces the sample to a more standardized state where that same underlying chemistry produces a more consistent color, which is why two mineral samples that look identical on the surface but leave different-colored streaks are best explained by different mineral identities or very different impurity histories, not testing error, since streak really is capturing something surface color often obscures.
