Velocity has both a size and a direction, and acceleration is defined as the
rate of change of either one. An object circling at perfectly constant speed
has an unchanging speed but a direction that never stops turning, and that
alone is enough to make its acceleration non-zero. Treating "constant speed"
as "no acceleration" confuses the size of velocity with velocity itself.

At any instant the velocity points tangent to the circle — along the direction
of travel right now, perpendicular to the line from the centre. It never
points toward or away from the centre; that would mean moving directly inward
or outward, which is exactly what circular motion is not doing. The
acceleration, by contrast, points toward the centre at every instant — it has
to, since its entire job is to bend the velocity vector back around the curve
rather than letting it fly off straight. This is centripetal acceleration, and
its perpendicularity to velocity is why it changes direction without ever
changing speed, the same logic that governs a magnetic force on a moving
charge.

Speed, radius and period are linked by the circle's circumference: one full
lap covers a distance 2πr in a time T, so v = 2πr/T. A larger v at fixed
radius means each lap is covered faster, so the period is *shorter*, not
longer — speed and period move in opposite directions when radius is held
fixed, which is worth checking against intuition before trusting a
calculation.
