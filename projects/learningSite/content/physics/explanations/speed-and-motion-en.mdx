Speed tells you how quickly something covers ground: divide the distance
travelled by the time it took, and that's it. A car covering 120 km in two
hours has an average speed of 60 km/h, regardless of whether it drove that
whole time at a steady pace or sped up and slowed down along the way.

That word _average_ matters. A speedometer shows instantaneous speed — the
speed right now — while distance divided by total time only ever gives you
the average over the whole trip. The two can be very different if the motion
isn't steady.

Speed is a scalar: it has a size but no direction. Two cars leaving the same
point at 60 km/h, one heading north and one heading south, have identical
speeds but are clearly not doing the same thing — that distinction is exactly
what velocity, a vector, is for.
