Assemble the three components of E and the three components of B into a single 4x4
antisymmetric matrix, the electromagnetic field tensor F, indexed by the four spacetime
coordinates. Under a Lorentz transformation to a different inertial frame, F transforms as a
genuine rank-2 tensor — meaning its new components in the boosted frame are fixed,
calculable mixtures of the old E and B components, rather than E and B transforming as
separate, independent objects.

This is the precise sense in which electric and magnetic fields are not two separate
things: what one observer calls a pure electric field (say, the field around a charge at
rest) is measured by an observer moving relative to that charge as a combination of electric
and magnetic fields, related by exactly the Lorentz transformation of F. Neither observer is
wrong; "the electric field" and "the magnetic field" are frame-dependent slices of one
frame-independent object, the electromagnetic field tensor, the same way space and time
separately are frame-dependent slices of one spacetime interval.

Written in terms of F, Maxwell's four vector equations collapse to just two tensor
equations: one that is automatically satisfied once the fields are derived from a
four-potential (encoding the two source-free equations, Gauss's law for magnetism and
Faraday's law, as a geometric identity rather than a separate physical statement) and one
that relates F's divergence to the four-current (encoding Gauss's law and the
Ampere-Maxwell law as a single equation). Both are explicitly, term-by-term the same
equation in every inertial frame — the covariant form makes Lorentz invariance a manifest
property of the equations' structure, not something that has to be checked case by case.
