Significant figures record how precisely a value is actually known. The
number 4.20 cm claims three significant figures of precision — the trailing
zero is deliberate, telling you the measurement was good to the nearest
hundredth of a centimetre. Write it as 4.2 cm instead, and you've quietly
thrown away that claim of precision, even though the numbers look almost
identical.

Zeros are the trickiest part of counting them. Leading zeros, as in 0.0042,
never count — they're just placeholders showing where the decimal point is.
Trailing zeros after a decimal point, as in 4.20, always count, because
there'd be no reason to write them otherwise.

The result of a calculation can never be more precise than its least precise
input — multiplying a length known to 2 significant figures by one known to
5 gives an answer that's only trustworthy to 2, no matter how many digits
a calculator happily displays.
