Momentum is the product of an object's mass and velocity — a heavy truck
rolling slowly and a light car speeding along can have the same momentum,
and both would take the same push to stop.

Impulse is what changes that momentum: it's the product of a force and how
long that force acts for, and it always equals the resulting change in
momentum. This is why airbags and crumple zones work — they don't reduce the
impulse needed to stop you (that's fixed by your mass and speed), they
stretch out the time over which it's delivered, which means a smaller force
is enough to produce it.

A gentle push held for a long time can produce exactly the same impulse, and
therefore the same change in momentum, as a hard shove applied for an
instant — force and time trade off against each other as long as their
product stays the same.
