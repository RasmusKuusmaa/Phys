Length contraction is time dilation seen from the other side. Take the
cosmic-ray muon again: in our frame it survives the trip because its clock
is stretched. In the muon's own frame its clock is perfectly normal and its
lifetime is the usual short one — yet it still reaches the ground. The only
way both accounts can describe the same events is if, in the muon's frame,
the atmosphere it has to cross is thinner. It is.

The contraction happens only along the direction of motion. A cube moving
along one of its edges is measured in the laboratory as a rectangular box,
shortened in the direction of travel and completely unchanged across it. A
sphere is measured as a flattened ellipsoid, not as a smaller sphere. This
directionality is the practical test of whether someone has understood the
effect or is picturing an object being crushed.

And nothing is being crushed. No force acts on the rod, its internal
structure is untouched, and in its own rest frame it has exactly the length
it always had — its *proper* length. Contraction is a statement about what
a measurement in a relatively moving frame yields, not about damage done to
the object. Ask "shorter according to whom?" and the apparent paradox
disappears: to the crew riding along, it is the laboratory that is short.
