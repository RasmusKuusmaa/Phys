A wheel spinning on a fixed axle is going nowhere, and yet stopping it takes
real work — so it plainly holds energy. That energy is ½Iω², the rotational
twin of ½mv², with moment of inertia standing in for mass and angular
velocity for speed. The two expressions are the same statement about motion,
written once for travelling and once for turning.

Because of the square, spin rate dominates. Double the angular velocity and
the stored energy quadruples; this is exactly why a flywheel is a serious
energy store and also why a burst one is dangerous. And because the moment
of inertia depends on how the mass is distributed, a flywheel is built heavy
at the rim: the same kilogram stores far more energy out there than near the
hub.

An object that rolls is doing both things at once, and its kinetic energy is
the sum: ½mv² for the centre of mass moving along, plus ½Iω² for the spin
about that centre. Miss the second term and rolling problems come out wrong.
It is the reason a ball released on a slope arrives slower than a
frictionless block released beside it — some of the gravitational energy went
into spinning it up rather than into speeding it along.
