A changing electric field creates a changing magnetic field, and that
changing magnetic field creates a changing electric field in turn — each one
regenerating the other as they go. This self-sustaining pair propagates
through space on its own as an electromagnetic wave, with no medium
required to carry it.

That last point sets electromagnetic waves apart from sound and water
waves, which genuinely do need a medium to travel through. Light reaching
Earth from the Sun has crossed 150 million kilometres of essentially empty
vacuum, something a sound wave simply cannot do.

Visible light is just one narrow slice of a much wider electromagnetic
spectrum that includes radio waves, microwaves, infrared, ultraviolet,
X-rays and gamma rays — all fundamentally the same kind of wave,
differing only in frequency and wavelength. Every one of them, from the
longest radio wave to the shortest gamma ray, travels at exactly the same
speed through a vacuum: the speed of light.
