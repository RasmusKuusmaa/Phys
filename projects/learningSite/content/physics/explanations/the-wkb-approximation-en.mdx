Write the wavefunction as an amplitude times a rapidly varying phase, psi = A(x) exp(i
S(x)/hbar), and substitute into the Schrodinger equation. If the potential — and hence the
local classical momentum p(x) — changes slowly over the distance of one de Broglie
wavelength, the leading-order result is that S(x) is just the classical action, the integral
of p(x) dx, and the amplitude A(x) varies as 1/sqrt(p(x)) — larger where the particle moves
slowly (spending more time there), smaller where it moves fast.

In a classically allowed region (where the total energy exceeds the potential), this gives
an oscillating wavefunction whose local wavelength tracks the local classical momentum; in a
classically forbidden region (potential exceeds total energy), the same substitution gives
an exponentially decaying or growing solution — the same as the ordinary tunneling picture,
but now with a position-dependent decay rate set by the local (imaginary) momentum, rather
than a single fixed decay constant, which is what lets WKB estimate tunneling through
barriers of essentially any shape, not just a flat rectangular one.

Matching the oscillating and decaying solutions correctly across the classical turning
points (where the WKB approximation itself technically breaks down, since the local
wavelength diverges there) recovers a quantization condition: the total accumulated phase
over one classical period must equal an integer (plus a fixed 1/2, from the turning-point
matching) times 2*pi — essentially the action-angle variable's Bohr-Sommerfeld condition,
but now derived as a genuine, controlled approximation to the Schrodinger equation rather
than an independent postulate, and correct to good accuracy whenever the underlying
slowly-varying-potential assumption actually holds.
