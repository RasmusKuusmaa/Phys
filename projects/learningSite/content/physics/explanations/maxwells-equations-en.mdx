Maxwell's four equations say, in order: electric field lines begin and end
on charge; magnetic field lines never begin or end at all, because isolated
magnetic poles do not exist; a changing magnetic field creates a circulating
electric field; and a magnetic field circulates around both electric current
*and* a changing electric field. The first three summarised what was already
known. The last clause of the fourth was Maxwell's own addition, and it
changed physics.

The reason it mattered is that it closed a loop. A changing electric field
makes a magnetic field, which as it changes makes an electric field, which
makes a magnetic field. Once started, the pair can sustain each other and
travel with no charges or currents present at all. Maxwell computed the
speed of this self-sustaining disturbance from two constants measured in
laboratory experiments on capacitors and coils, and got the speed of light —
which is how optics turned out to be a branch of electromagnetism.

That also settles what electromagnetic waves travel *through*: nothing. Each
field is the source of the other, so the wave carries its own means of
propagation and needs no medium. The nineteenth century spent decades
searching for an ether anyway, and Michelson and Morley's failure to find it
is one of the results special relativity was built on. Together the four
equations cover radio, radiant heat, visible light, X-rays and gamma rays as
one phenomenon at different frequencies.
