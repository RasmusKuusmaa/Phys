Temperature and heat get used interchangeably in everyday speech, but they
describe genuinely different things. Temperature is a measure of how hot or
cold something is — a property an object simply has. Heat is energy on the
move, flowing from a hotter object to a colder one because of a temperature
difference between them.

A large pot of lukewarm water and a small cup of very hot tea can contain
the same total amount of thermal energy, or even have the water hold more,
despite the tea being at a much higher temperature — temperature doesn't
care how much material there is, but the total energy stored very much does.

Once two objects reach the same temperature, heat flow between them stops,
even though both still have plenty of thermal energy. That's the point of
"same temperature": it means there's no more temperature difference left to
drive further heat flow, not that either object has run out of energy.
