A charge's electric and magnetic fields don't update instantaneously everywhere the moment
it moves — no signal travels faster than light, so the field at a distant point at time t is
determined by the charge's position and motion at the earlier "retarded" time t minus
(distance)/c, not by where the charge actually is right now. Solving Maxwell's equations with
this retardation built in gives the retarded potentials, and from them, the fields of an
arbitrarily moving point charge.

For a charge moving at constant velocity, the resulting field is just the same
electrostatic-like field, evaluated at the (correctly retarded) present position — no
genuine radiation, since a uniformly moving charge's field can always be transformed away
into a plain Coulomb field by switching to its rest frame. It's specifically the
acceleration-dependent piece of the retarded field, present only when the charge's velocity
is actually changing, that falls off as 1/r rather than 1/r^2 and carries energy away to
infinity — true electromagnetic radiation.

The Larmor formula packages the total power radiated by that acceleration-dependent field
into one simple result: P is proportional to q^2 times a^2 (charge squared times
acceleration squared), with no dependence on the charge's actual velocity. This is why a
charge oscillating back and forth (as in a radio antenna) radiates continuously, why a
charge moving at constant velocity in a straight line radiates nothing at all, and why
classical physics predicted that an orbiting electron should spiral into the nucleus — an
orbiting charge is a continuously accelerating charge, radiating away energy — a real and
historically important failure of purely classical atomic physics that only quantum
mechanics resolved.
