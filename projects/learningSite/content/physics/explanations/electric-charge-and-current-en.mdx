Electric charge comes in exactly two flavours, positive and negative, and
like charges repel while opposite charges attract. Nearly everything you
see charged up in daily life — a balloon rubbed on hair, a phone battery, a
lightning bolt — comes down to an imbalance between these two.

An electric current is simply charge on the move: a steady flow of charge
through a conductor, measured in amperes as the amount of charge passing a
point each second. In most everyday circuits it's electrons doing the
moving, drifting slowly through the wire's atomic lattice.

Current doesn't get "used up" as it flows around a circuit, even though
components along the way certainly use up energy. Measure the current
before a resistor and after it, in a simple series circuit, and you'll get
the same number both times — what changes across the resistor is voltage,
not current.
