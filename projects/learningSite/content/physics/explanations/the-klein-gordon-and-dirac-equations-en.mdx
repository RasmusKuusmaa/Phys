The Schrodinger equation treats time and space asymmetrically — first-order in time,
second-order in space — which cannot be relativistically correct, since special relativity
requires time and space to be treated on an equal footing. The most direct fix, replacing
energy and momentum with their operator forms in the relativistic energy-momentum relation
E^2 = (pc)^2 + (mc^2)^2, gives the Klein-Gordon equation: second-order in both space and
time, and correctly relativistic, but it describes a spin-0 particle and — because it's
second-order in time — allows solutions with formally negative probability density, a
serious problem for a single-particle wave equation.

Dirac's approach instead insisted on an equation that is first-order in time from the start,
which forced it to also be first-order in space to remain consistent with relativity —
achievable only by promoting the coefficients in the equation to matrices rather than plain
numbers, and correspondingly promoting the wavefunction to a multi-component object (a
spinor) rather than a single complex number. Working through the consequences of this
structure shows that the extra components describe intrinsic angular momentum automatically:
electron spin-1/2 falls directly out of demanding a relativistically consistent, first-order
wave equation, rather than needing to be added to the theory by hand the way it does in
nonrelativistic quantum mechanics.

The Dirac equation's negative-energy solutions, initially a puzzling mathematical byproduct,
turned out to be a genuine physical prediction rather than a flaw: Dirac proposed, and
experiment soon confirmed with Anderson's 1932 discovery of the positron, that these
solutions describe antiparticles — for the electron, a positively charged twin with
identical mass. This was one of theoretical physics's most striking successes: a purely
mathematical consistency requirement, imposed to satisfy special relativity, predicted an
entirely new form of matter that had never been observed at the time the equation was
written down.
