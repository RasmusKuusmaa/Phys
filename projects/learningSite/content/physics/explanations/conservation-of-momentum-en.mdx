In any collision or interaction where no outside force gets involved, the
total momentum of everything involved is exactly the same afterwards as it
was before. It doesn't matter whether the objects bounce apart or crumple
together — momentum is conserved either way.

What isn't guaranteed is kinetic energy. In a perfectly elastic collision,
like two billiard balls colliding, both momentum and kinetic energy are
conserved. In an inelastic collision, like a car crash where the vehicles
stick together, momentum is still conserved, but a lot of kinetic energy is
converted into heat, sound, and the work of crumpling metal.

A useful way to picture "no outside force": a system of exploding fireworks
has momentum zero before the explosion and momentum zero after, because
every fragment flying off in one direction is balanced by other fragments
flying off in a way that cancels it out — the explosion is entirely
internal to the system.
