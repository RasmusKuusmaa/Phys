A Brownian particle suspended in fluid is buffeted constantly by countless molecular collisions, far too numerous and far too fast to track individually. Rather than abandoning any equation of motion for the particle, the Langevin equation writes one down anyway: an ordinary Newtonian equation with a deterministic friction (drag) force proportional to the particle's velocity, plus an additional random force term representing the net, unresolved effect of all those molecular collisions. This random force is not a mathematical convenience but a genuinely physical term -- it has zero average value and decorrelates extremely quickly in time, exactly the statistical signature countless independent molecular impacts should produce -- and the resulting equation is a genuine stochastic differential equation, since its solution (the particle's trajectory) is itself random, different every time the equation is solved with a different realization of the random force.

The Fokker-Planck equation offers the complementary large-scale view of the same physics. Instead of tracking one particle's individual, unpredictable path, it describes how the probability distribution over an entire ensemble of particles' possible positions (and velocities) changes over time, and this description is completely deterministic: given the distribution at one time, the Fokker-Planck equation predicts the distribution at every later time exactly, with no randomness left at the level of the distribution itself. The equation includes both a drift term (describing systematic, average motion, such as sedimentation under gravity) and a diffusion term (describing the spreading-out effect of the underlying random buffeting), generalizing the simpler diffusion equation to include systematic drift as well as pure diffusion.

These are not two competing descriptions of Brownian motion but two exactly equivalent views of the same physics at different levels of description. Solve the Langevin equation numerically many times, each with an independently generated random force realization, and average the resulting ensemble of individual trajectories together, and the result reproduces precisely the same evolving probability distribution the Fokker-Planck equation predicts directly. Randomness at the individual-trajectory level and determinism at the probability-distribution level coexist without contradiction -- exactly the same relationship that lets the deterministic diffusion equation successfully describe an ensemble of individually unpredictable random walks.
