Interference is what happens when two or more waves overlap: at each point,
their displacements simply add together. Where two crests meet, they add to
make a bigger crest — constructive interference. Where a crest meets a
trough of equal size, they cancel out completely — destructive interference.

That cancellation doesn't destroy any energy, even though it might look
that way at first. Energy that seems to vanish at a point of destructive
interference reappears at nearby points of constructive interference, where
the waves add up to more than either alone — interference redistributes
energy, it never deletes it.

Diffraction is a related but distinct idea: it's the spreading of a wave as
it passes through an opening or around an obstacle, most noticeable when the
gap is comparable in size to the wavelength. It happens at edges and
obstacles too, not only through gaps — sound bending around a corner so you
can hear someone before you see them is diffraction at work.
