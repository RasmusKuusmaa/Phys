Newton's second law connects three quantities: the net force acting on an
object, its mass, and the acceleration that force produces.

Double the force on the same mass, and the acceleration doubles too. Double
the mass while keeping the force fixed, and the acceleration is cut in
half — mass measures an object's resistance to being accelerated, its
inertia.

The law only accounts for the _net_ force. If two equal forces pull in
opposite directions, they cancel, and the acceleration is zero even though
individual forces are still acting.
