Uric acid, the body's end product of purine breakdown, dissolves in blood and other body fluids only up to a certain concentration -- exactly the same solubility-limit chemistry that governs whether calcium oxalate or uric acid stays dissolved in urine or precipitates into a kidney stone. When blood urate concentration climbs chronically above that solubility limit, the excess doesn't stay in solution indefinitely; it eventually precipitates as monosodium urate crystals. Most of the time this chronic elevation comes from the kidneys excreting less urate than they should, not from diet alone, though purine-rich food and alcohol can push an already-elevated level further over the threshold.

What happens next is what distinguishes gout from a kidney stone using the same underlying chemistry: instead of precipitating in the urinary tract, urate crystals form within a joint space, most classically the base of the big toe. Resident immune cells patrolling the joint recognize these crystals as a danger signal -- not because they're foreign in the sense a pathogen is, but because their physical, needle-like structure triggers the same innate-immune alarm pathways that respond to other kinds of tissue damage. The result is an intense, rapid inflammatory cascade: within hours, the joint becomes hot, swollen, and severely painful, a speed of onset that has no parallel in osteoarthritis's yearslong mechanical-wear process or even rheumatoid arthritis's more gradual autoimmune course.

Because the attack is a direct reaction to physical crystals rather than an infection, antibiotics do nothing for it, and the attack itself will resolve once the acute inflammatory response runs its course, whether or not the crystals are fully cleared. But the crystals that caused this attack came from a chronic chemical state -- urate concentration sitting above its solubility limit -- and that state doesn't resolve on its own between attacks. Long-term management therefore targets exactly the chemistry that started the whole process: lowering blood urate concentration reliably below the point where new crystals can form, the same logic used to prevent recurrent kidney stones from the same underlying compound.
